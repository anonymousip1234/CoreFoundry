## Optional Exploration

Five small, hand-picked resources. **No more than two are marked
Recommended** — the rest are genuinely optional, pick only what
interests you.

### Resource 1 — Teachable Machine

- **Source:** Google Creative Lab
- **Type:** Free interactive tool (browser-based, no account needed)
- **Time:** ~15 minutes
- **Level:** Beginner — no coding required
- **Priority:** ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **Link:** https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com/

**Why look at it:** This lets you *actually do* what we described in
class — give a computer examples (photos, sounds, or poses), and watch
it build something from them that can then guess on something new.
It's the `DATA → LEARNING → MODEL → PREDICTION` pipeline, hands-on, in
your browser.

**Focus on:** Training a simple image classifier with your webcam
(e.g., two hand gestures) and noticing how the model's guess changes
as you give it more or different examples.

**Skip:** The "export your model" / website-integration features —
that's a later, technical step, not needed here.

---

### Resource 2 — AI for Oceans

- **Source:** Code.org (nonprofit, official)
- **Type:** Free interactive activity
- **Time:** ~15–20 minutes
- **Level:** Beginner — no coding required
- **Priority:** ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **Link:** https://code.org/oceans

**Why look at it:** You train a system to sort "fish" from "trash" by
giving it examples — then you can deliberately give it bad or narrow
examples and watch it make worse guesses. It's a very close hands-on
match to this class's cats-vs-dogs reasoning and the data-quality idea
("poor data → poor predictions").

**Focus on:** What happens to the system's guesses when you feed it
narrow or one-sided examples on purpose.

**Skip:** Nothing technical to skip — it's built for beginners
throughout. (It does use the word "bias" briefly; that's not a term
this class requires, but it's fine to notice it here.)

### Resource 4 — Google Maps 101: How AI Helps Predict Traffic and Determine Routes

- **Source:** Google (official blog)
- **Type:** Short article
- **Time:** ~8 minutes
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Priority:** Optional
- **Link:** https://blog.google/products/maps/google-maps-101-how-ai-helps-predict-traffic-and-determine-routes/

**Why look at it:** A plain-language look at how Maps combines
historical traffic patterns with live data to predict your ETA — a
real-world "AI Around Us" example, in the company's own words.

**Focus on:** The idea of learning from historical patterns *plus*
current data to make a prediction.

**Skip:** The single brief mention of "Graph Neural Networks" — that's
a later, technical topic, not needed for this class.

## A Note on How to Use These

**Don't try to understand everything.** If you open one of these and
it starts feeling technical, that's your cue to stop, not a sign you
should push through. Before going deeper into anything AI-related
online, ask yourself:

- Does this reinforce something from class, or is it introducing a new
  technical topic?
- Do I already understand the basic idea here, or am I skimming past
  confusion?

If the answer is "this has gone technical" — stop there. You've gotten
what you need from it, and the rest is for a later class.
