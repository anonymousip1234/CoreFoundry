## Question 1 — The Rule Problem (3 points)

A college wants to build a simple system that automatically checks
ID-card photo submissions and decides whether each photo is
"acceptable" — for example, no sunglasses, a plain background, facing
the camera — **using only rules a person writes by hand.**

**As more students submit photos, in more lighting conditions, poses,
and styles, why might this hand-written-rules approach become
difficult to manage?**

Explain the role of variety, exceptions, and changing situations in
your answer.

## Question 4 — Traditional Programming vs. Machine Learning (3 points)

A college is deciding how to automatically sort photos submitted to
its event page into **"Indoor"** or **"Outdoor."**

**Approach A:** A developer sits down and writes a list of explicit
conditions describing what counts as "Indoor" versus "Outdoor."

**Approach B:** The system is given many examples of photos already
labeled "Indoor" or "Outdoor," and uses a learning process to build a
model that can make a prediction about new, unlabeled photos.

**a.** Which approach is traditional programming?

**b.** Which approach is Machine Learning?

**c.** In your own words, what is the key difference between the two
approaches?

## Question 6 — Data Quality / An Unseen Case (3 points)

A campus system is learning to guess whether a parked vehicle outside
a building is **"a student's ride"** or **"a delivery vehicle."**
During learning, it only ever saw two kinds of examples: small
bicycles (labeled "student's ride") and large delivery vans (labeled
"delivery vehicle"). One day, it encounters a food-delivery worker's
**motorbike** parked outside.

**What might happen, and why?**

Connect your answer to what the system did or didn't see while
learning.
