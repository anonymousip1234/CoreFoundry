# Assignment 1 — Instructor Grading Workflow

**Bong Study Hub — Foundation Batch 2026 · Class 01: Welcome to Computer Science**

A simple, repeatable process for grading each submission. Budget
**~3–5 minutes per submission** once you're familiar with the rubric —
this is a beginner assignment, not a document requiring deep review.

---

## The 8-Step Process

### 1. Receive submission
Accept any of the three formats (handwritten/photographed, typed
document, PDF) without penalty for format choice. If a submission is
missing the required structure entirely (no labeled Name/Process/Input/
etc.), ask the student to resubmit in the template format rather than
guessing at their intent.

### 2. Check I/P/O
Read the Input, Process, and Output sections first, before the
algorithm. Ask yourself: does this triple actually describe the chosen
process, and are the three parts genuinely distinct from each other (not
one thing repeated three ways)?

### 3. Check the algorithm
Read the numbered steps in order, as if you were the literal computer
from the tea activity — doing exactly and only what's written. Note any
point where you'd have to guess, assume something, or where the order
doesn't make sense.

### 4. Apply the rubric
Score each of the 5 dimensions in `03_Grading_Rubric.md` (2 points each,
10 total). If the Optional Challenge was attempted, do not add its
quality into the numeric score — acknowledge it in written feedback
instead.

### 5. Give one positive comment
Pick (or adapt) one comment from `04_Feedback_Comments.md` that names
something the student did well, specifically — not a generic "good job."

### 6. Give one improvement suggestion
Pick (or adapt) one constructive comment that points at the single
biggest gap — not every gap. A beginner benefits more from fixing one
thing clearly than from a list of five minor notes.

### 7. Record score
Log the score (out of 10) and your two comments (positive +
improvement) in your gradebook or tracking sheet. Keep a copy of
particularly strong or particularly struggling submissions as reference
points for calibrating later batches.

### 8. Identify students needing support
Flag a student for a quick follow-up (in the next class, or a short
1:1 message) if **any** of the following apply:
- Score is 4/10 or below.
- The algorithm has fewer than 3 usable, ordered steps.
- Input/Process/Output are confused with each other (not just
  incomplete, but conceptually swapped).
- The student submitted nothing, or submitted something unrelated to the
  assignment.

Do not flag a student solely for skipping the Optional Challenge, for
messy handwriting, or for imperfect English — none of those indicate a
conceptual gap in this assignment's actual learning objective.

---

## Calibration Tip

Before grading a full batch, grade 3–5 submissions first and compare
your scores against each other. If your scores for similar-quality work
drift by more than ~2 points across that sample, re-read
`03_Grading_Rubric.md`'s dimension descriptors once more before
continuing — consistency matters more than precision here.

## After Grading

- Note any pattern across the batch (e.g., "many students confused
  process with output") — this is useful input for how you open the next
  class, and pairs well with the Instructor Reflection Checklist in the
  Master Instructor Guide.
- Return feedback within a timeframe students can still act on before
  the next related assignment — for a beginner-confidence assignment
  like this one, faster feedback matters more than exhaustive feedback.
