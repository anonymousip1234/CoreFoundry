## What This Assignment Is About

In class, we learned that solving a problem with a computer follows a
chain:

```
PROBLEM → LOGIC → ALGORITHM → PROGRAM → RESULT
```

We also learned that almost anything can be described as:

```
INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT
```

And that an **algorithm** — a finite sequence of clear steps used to
solve a problem — is not the same thing as a **program**. An algorithm
is the recipe; a program is the dish, written in a specific programming
language.

This assignment asks you to do the "recipe" part only. **You will not
write any code.** You will pick something you already do in everyday
life, and describe it the way a computer would need it described: with
no missing assumptions, in clear order, step by step — exactly like the
"Teach a Computer How to Make Tea" activity we did in class.

## Your Task

**Step 1 — Choose ONE everyday process** from the list below (or propose
your own to your instructor):

- ATM withdrawal
- Ordering food
- Booking a cab
- Online shopping
- Making tea
- Sending a message
- Booking a train ticket
- Going to college

**Step 2 — Identify:**

1. **INPUT** — what information or materials are needed before anything happens.
2. **PROCESS** — what actually happens, step by step, in general terms.
3. **OUTPUT** — what the result is at the end.

**Step 3 — Write an algorithm** for your chosen process with **at least
5 clear, numbered steps**, in plain language.

**Rules:**
- No code. No programming syntax. Plain sentences only.
- Each step should describe one clear action.
- Steps must be in the order they actually happen.
- Write it precisely enough that someone who has never done this task
  could follow it without guessing.

## Beginner Example (Fully Worked)

This example shows exactly the level of detail expected. You may not
copy this process (choose a different one from the list), but match this
style.

**Chosen process:** Ordering food online

**INPUT:** Restaurant, food item, quantity, delivery address, payment method

**PROCESS:** Selecting a restaurant, selecting food, adding items to the
cart, entering the delivery address, choosing a payment method,
confirming the order

**OUTPUT:** Order placed successfully

**Algorithm:**

1. Open the food ordering app.
2. Choose a restaurant.
3. Select the food items you want and choose the quantity of each.
4. Add the selected items to your cart.
5. Enter or confirm your delivery address.
6. Choose a payment method.
7. Review your order and total price.
8. Confirm the order.
9. Receive confirmation that the order has been placed.

Notice: 9 steps, one clear action each, no missing assumptions, no code.

## Optional Challenge — Not Required

**This section is entirely optional.** It will not be held against you
if you skip it, and it is not required to score full marks. Attempt it
only if you want to push your thinking further.

If you'd like the challenge, add to your algorithm:

- **At least two decisions** — a point where what happens next depends
  on a condition.
- **At least one possible failure** — something that could go wrong.
- **At least one assumption** your algorithm quietly makes.
- **At least one edge case** — an unusual situation your algorithm
  should still be able to handle.

You do not need to know any programming syntax (no "if/else") — just
describe the decision in plain English.

**Example (continuing the food-ordering algorithm above):**

> **Payment successful?**
> **YES** → Order is confirmed, and a confirmation message is sent.
> **NO** → Payment retry is offered; if it fails again, the order is not
> confirmed and the cart is saved for later.
>
> **Assumption my algorithm makes:** That the delivery address entered is
> serviceable by the restaurant.
>
> **Edge case:** What if the item selected is out of stock after you've
> already added it to your cart?
